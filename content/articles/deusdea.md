The term **deusdea** {{small|printed *Deusdia* in DanMachi 1, 3, 4, 5 and 8}} designates [[races#god|gods and goddesses]] as beings transcending mortals.[@fm01-designation, fm11-contrast] [[bell-cranell|Bell]] describes [[hestia|Hestia]] as a deusdea existing on a different level from humans, demi-humans and Dungeon monsters.[@fm01-designation] He also uses the designation when reflecting on the gap between gods and the inhabitants of the mortal world.[@fm11-contrast]

## Uses in context {#uses-in-context}

| Volume | Events |
|---|---|
| DanMachi 7 | [[mikoto|Mikoto]] stands protectively before [[chigusa|Chigusa]] but struggles to find words when confronted by the deusdea.[@fm07-words] |
| Sword Oratoria 5 | The explanation of adventurer aliases uses the term for the gods whose recognition those names signify.[@so05-aliases] |
| Sword Oratoria 11 | [[dionysus|Dionysus]] thinks that, as a deusdea, he cannot be deceived by powers of the mortal realm.[@so11-belief] |
| Sword Oratoria 14 | [[riveria|Riveria]]'s reflections describe the deusdea descending to the [[tenkai-and-gekai|mortal realm]] as parents of its inhabitants.[@so14-reflection] |
| Familia Chronicle 2 | [[freya|Freya]] argues that nothing unclean comes from a deusdea while discussing bathing in an oasis.[@fc02-dialogue] |


[@fm01-designation]: FM01 | Chapter 1: World, Reality, and Desire | Bell describes Hestia as a transcendent being. The Japanese edition (file cFB, paragraphs 61–62) calls her a transcendent being one dimension apart from humans, demi-humans and the Dungeon's monsters.
[@fm11-contrast]: FM11 | Chapter 1: The White Rabbit Brought Low | Bell contrasts gods with mortal inhabitants.
[@fm07-words]: FM07 | Chapter 2: Run, Cranell | Mikoto shields Chigusa and struggles for words before gods. The Japanese edition (file c1BC, paragraph 114) says she is at a loss for words before them.
[@so05-aliases]: SO05 | Interlude: Flip Side of the Farce | Aliases signify recognition by gods.
[@so11-belief]: SO11 | Chapter 6: And Then the God Smiled | Dionysus believes his divine status prevents mortal deception.
[@fc02-dialogue]: FC02 | Ali and the 8 Followers | Freya invokes divine perfection in an argument about bathing.
[@so14-reflection]: SO14 | Chapter 2: The High Elf’s Departure | Gods are described as parents of mortal inhabitants. The Japanese edition (file part0020, paragraph 27) calls the transcendent beings the fathers of the mortal world's people.
[@fm01-deusdea-ja]: FM01 | Chapter 1: World, Reality, and Desire | The Japanese edition writes the word in kanji meaning transcendent being, with the reading deusdea (file cFB, paragraph 62). The English prints Deusdia in DanMachi 1, 3, 4, 5 and 8 (6 lines) and deusdea in DanMachi 7 and later and in the other series (27 lines in 18 volumes).
